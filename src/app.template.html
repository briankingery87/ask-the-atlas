How the Argument Score works
One point per metric. No weighting, no hidden thumb on the scale. The comparison is the point; the number is just the scoreboard, and you can audit it at a glance.
Both sides must have a real value. A placeholder never beats a number, so a well-documented FBS program cannot out-score a thinly documented D3 program on paperwork alone. Ties score for neither side.
The live-metric count is the honest part. An 8-2 across twelve live metrics is an argument. A 1-0 across one metric is a coin flip with extra steps, and the card says so.
Direction is declared per metric. Lower wins on poll rank, recruiting class and road miles. Earlier wins on year built. Higher wins on everything else. Hover any row to see which.

Question 5 Ranked by interest, not by kickoff time

What should I actually watch?
Every game of the season, week by week, scored 0-100 on a transparent watchability index. Opens on the current week; pick any other week and the day filter rebuilds to match. Components that have no data this week are dropped from the denominator instead of scored as zero, and each card shows what earned its score.
How the Watchability Score works
Seven components, each 0-1, each weighted. The score is the weighted mean of the components that have data for that game - not of all seven.
Missing data leaves the denominator. A game with no published line loses drama and fireworks from the divisor rather than scoring zero on them, so a Tuesday game the sportsbooks ignored is not punished for their silence.
Every card shows its working. The line under each score names the two or three components that actually carried it, so a 71 is never just a number.

What Ask the Atlas is

The CFB Atlas has two halves that share one data model. The Experience is the configurable half - ArcGIS Experience Builder, nine web maps, four dashboards, Arcade popups. You pick a view, toggle layers, click a feature. It is the right tool for exploring a theme and it is where the cartography lives.

Ask the Atlas is the custom half. It reads the same published services through their REST endpoints and does the thing configuration cannot: joins across services, distance math, ad-hoc filtering, and composite indexes computed in the browser. Five questions, one page each.

Neither replaces the other. The Experience answers "show me the fan territory layer." Ask the Atlas answers "whose country do I live in."

Data honesty live counts, this panel empties itself as the season fills in

The Atlas never writes null - it writes typed placeholders, so every attribute always renders. That is the right call for popups and a trap for anything that does math. This panel counts how many rows carry a real value per field, straight off the live services. Anything in orange is published but not yet populated, and every page here treats those fields as absent rather than as zero.

Placeholder vocabulary in force across all services: -1, -1.0, -999, -999.0, empty string, N/A, TBD, Pending, and any string beginning N/A - .

The three indexes every composite on this site, with its working shown

Argument Score Tale of the Tape

A deliberately dumb index, because the comparison is the point and the number is just the scoreboard. Fifteen metrics, one point each, no weights. A metric is only played when both programs carry a real value for it, which is what keeps a well-documented FBS program from beating a D3 program on paperwork alone. Ties are scored for neither side. The live-metric count is published next to the score so you can see how thin the argument actually is.

FamilyMetrics
StandingCoaches poll, poll points, 2025 win pct, 5-year win pct
RatingFPI, SP+, Elo, talent composite, recruiting class
PlaceStadium seats, year built, elevation, season road miles
TerritoryEmpire population, empire counties, battlegrounds held

Direction is declared per metric. Lower wins on rank, recruiting class and road miles; earlier wins on year built; higher wins on everything else.

Watchability Score The Slate

A weighted mean, 0-100, over only the components that have data for that game. Missing components leave the denominator; they are never scored as zero. That single decision is what makes the index usable in August, when only a quarter of the slate has a published betting line.

ComponentWeightReads
Star power3.0Poll rank of both sides, AP first then Coaches
Drama3.0Closeness of the spread, flat above 21 points

Each card names the components that carried its score. Sorting by watchability is not the same as sorting by spread - a 38-point mismatch in a 100,000 seat stadium between two ranked teams still outranks a pick-em between two teams nobody is watching.

Gauntlet Index The Gauntlet

Strength of schedule, 0-100, built from the full published season rather than from a rating service. For each opponent the index takes the better of two signals - the opponent's FPI, and a bonus for a top-25 poll position - then adjusts for venue, because a true road game is worth more than the same opponent at home.

TermTreatment
Opponent strengthFPI normalized across the rated field; unrated opponents are excluded from the mean and counted separately
Ranked opponentsEach top-25 opponent adds a fixed bonus, scaled by rank
VenueRoad game ×1.10, neutral ×1.00, home ×0.92
UnratedReported as a count, never averaged in as zero - an FCS-heavy slate reads as thin data, not as an easy schedule

Preseason, FPI is the only rating CFBD has published, so the index leans on it. When SP+ and Elo land, the opponent-strength term picks them up automatically.

Services this page reads all public, all read-only

Six hosted feature services, published from ArcGIS Pro notebooks and refreshed by a scheduled task every Sunday. Item pages open in ArcGIS Online; endpoints are the REST layer this page actually queries.

Why it is built this way

Five decisions that shaped Ask the Atlas, each one worth stealing for anything else built on this data model.

  • Place-first entry. "What does college football look like where I live" is the question the county gravity model uniquely answers, and it needs to be one search box rather than three layers assembled by hand.
  • Cross-division nearest-neighbour. D2 and D3 are reference-only in the tiering rule, which makes 397 rows look like filler. Distance-from-me is the context where a D3 school twelve miles away beats an SEC school four hundred miles away.
  • Ad-hoc query as a first-class view. Dashboards answer questions you anticipated. A screener answers the ones you did not, and the presets are the part people share.
  • Score, then show the score's working. Any composite index should publish its components, its weights and its denominator on the same screen as the number.
  • Degrade loudly. Rigorous placeholder handling is invisible when it works. Surfacing it as a live panel turns a data gap into a credibility signal.

Where this goes next

Ask the Atlas is built to grow with the season. Each of these lights up on its own as the underlying services fill in - no rebuild required, because the page reads the data live and hides what is still placeholder.

  • Results and records - the schedule table carries scores and a completed flag. Week 1 flips them on, and the Gauntlet turns from a preview into a running tally.
  • The AP poll - only the Coaches Poll has published so far. Rank pills, the poll strip and star power all read AP first and fall back to Coaches, so AP appears the moment it lands.
  • SP+, Elo and SRS - the Ratings table already has the columns; CFBD has not filled them for 2026. The Tale of the Tape has the rows waiting and greyed.
  • Recruiting - 3,051 mapped commits and 2,139 origin-destination flows are published and unused here so far. A "where does the talent come from" page is the obvious next question.
  • The Rewind - a Sunday-morning counterpart to the Slate: what actually happened, biggest upsets against the line, and which counties changed hands.
